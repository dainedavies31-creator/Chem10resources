Kinetic Molecular theory

Proposed by James Clerk Maxwell and Ludwig Boltzmann to explain the general properties of gases.
    The postulates of this theory are:
  • Gases are made up of minute particles (atoms/molecules).
  • The attractive force between gaseous molecules is very low.
  • As the molecules are so far apart, the volume of gaseous molecules is negligible in comparison with the total volume of the gas.
  • The volume can be reduced by reducing the distance between the gaseous molecules.
  • Gaseous molecules are in constant motion in all directions. As a result, the molecules collide with one another and with the walls of the container. The force produced due to the collision of molecules with the walls of the container results in gaseous pressure.
  • Collisions of gaseous molecules are elastic in nature. That means, the kinetic energy of the molecules before and after the collision will be same.
  • The average kinetic energy of the gaseous molecules is directly proportional to its temperature.

General properties of gases

Volume

The space occupied by a substance is taken as it's volume.
If a liquid filled in a bottle of volume 1L is transferred to a bottle of volume 2 L, what will be its volume?
Ans: 1L
If oxygen gas filled in a bottle of volume 1L is transferred to a bottle of volume 2 L, what will be its volume?
Ans: 2L
What if a gas of volume 2 L is transferred to a bottle of 10 L volume?
Ans: 10L
The volume of a gas is the volume in which it is occupied.
1000cm³ = 1000mL = 1L

Pressure

We have seen that gaseous molecules are in constant random motion. As a result, they collide with one another and with the walls of the container. Due to these collisions, a force is experienced on the surface. Gaseous pressure is the force experienced per unit surface area.
Pressure = Force/Surface area
P = F/A
Generally, pressure is expressed in terms of atmospheric pressure (atm). You have learnt about atmospheric pressure in lower classes. The SI unit of pressure is Pascal (Pa). (Pa = N/m² or Newton per square meter).
1 atm = 1.01325 x105 Pa.

Temperature

Which is the energy acquired by molecules due to its movement? (Kinetic energy / potential energy)
Ans: Kinetic energy
What happens to the kinetic energy of the molecules if a gas is heated? (Increases / decreases)
Ans: Increases
When gases are heated, the energy of molecules increases and hence temperature also increases.
The SI unit of temperature is Kelvin(K). To convert the common unit oC to Kelvin, add 273 to it.

At constant temperature, the volume of a fixed mass of gas is inversely proportional to its pressure.
That is, V α 1P (temperature, mass constant)
V = a constant × 1P
PV = k (k = a constant)
    Practical examples of Boyle's law:
  • When a syringe piston is pushed inward, the pressure increases and the volume of air inside decreases.
  • A scuba diver notices air bubbles becoming larger as they rise to the surface because water pressure decreases.
  • Air inside a bicycle pump gets compressed when the handle is pushed down, reducing the volume of the gas.
  • Marshmallows expand inside a vacuum chamber because the surrounding pressure decreases.
  • When we breathe in, the lungs expand, decreasing pressure inside them and allowing air to enter.

b) Charle's law - temperature and volume of a gas.

Let us do an experiment (Fig. 4.9). Make a small hole on the lid of a glass bottle and fix an empty refill into it. Add a small drop of ink into the refill, then blow gently to move the ink to the centre of the refill. Cover the bottle with your palms.
What do you observe?
Ans: The ink drop rises.
Upto which point of the arrangement does air occupy?
Ans: It is from the bottom of the bottle to that part of the refill that contains the ink.
What change occurs to the temperature of the air inside the bottle when covered the bottle with palms?
Ans: The temperature inside the bottle increases.
Does the volume of the gas increase or decrease during this period?
Ans: Increase
It is now clear why the ink rises up in the refill.
Place the bottle in water and cool it. What do you observe?
Ans: The temperature inside the bottle decreases.
What happens to the volume of the gas when the bottle is cooled?
Ans: The volume decreases.
At constant pressure, the volume of a fixed mass of gas is directly proportional to its temperature.

V α T
V = kT
V/T = constant
    Practical examples of Charles' law:
  • A hot air balloon rises because the heated air inside expands.
  • A football kept in the sun expands slightly because the air inside becomes hotter.
  • A balloon shrinks when placed in a refrigerator because the temperature decreases.

Mole Concept

One mole is the amount of substance that contains 6.022 × 1023 particles (Avogadro Number).
1 mole of atoms = 6.022 × 1023 atoms
1 mole of molecules = 6.022 × 1023 molecules

Relative Atomic Mass and Mole

Relative atomic mass (Ar) of an element is the average mass of its atoms compared to 1/12th the mass of a carbon-12 atom.
Carbon-12 is taken as the standard reference.

Mass of 1 carbon-12 atom = 12 atomic mass units (u)

Atomic Mass Unit (u)

1 atomic mass unit (1u) = 1/12 of the mass of one carbon-12 atom.

Relation Between Relative Atomic Mass and Mole

The numerical value of relative atomic mass in grams is equal to the mass of one mole of that element.
Relative atomic mass (Ar) = Mass of 1 mole of atoms (in grams)

Why Atomic Mass is a Fraction?

The atomic mass of elements is calculated by considering the average mass of their isotopes based on their natural abundance.

Example 1: Neon

The natural abundance of neon isotopes is:

20Ne → 90.48%
21Ne → 0.27%
22Ne → 9.25%
Average atomic mass =
[(20 × 90.48) + (21 × 0.27) + (22 × 9.25)] ÷ 100
= 20.18 u

Example 2: Chlorine

The natural abundance of chlorine isotopes:

Cl-35 → 75%
Cl-37 → 25%
Average atomic mass =
[(35 × 75) + (37 × 25)] ÷ 100
= 3550 ÷ 100
= 35.5 u
The atomic mass of most elements is a fraction because it is the weighted average of the masses of all naturally occurring isotopes.

Molar Mass

Molar mass is the mass of one mole of a substance.
It is expressed in grams per mole (g mol⁻¹).
Molar Mass = Mass of substance (g) ÷ Number of moles (mol)

Relation with Relative Atomic Mass

The numerical value of relative atomic mass is equal to the molar mass in grams.
If Ar of Oxygen = 16
Molar mass of Oxygen = 16 g mol⁻¹

Molar Mass of Compounds

The molar mass of a compound is the sum of the atomic masses of all atoms present in its formula.
Molar mass of H2O
= (2 × 1) + (1 × 16)
= 18 g mol⁻¹
